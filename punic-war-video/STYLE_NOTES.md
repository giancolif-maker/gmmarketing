# Style notes — what makes the reference format work

Studied: OverSimplified "The Second Punic War" Parts 1–3 and "First Punic War"
(storyboard thumbnails + Part 1 captions). These notes record *techniques*, not
content: no jokes, lines, character names or art are reused from the channel.
OverSimplified already published a ~1-hour Second Punic War trilogy; ours is an
original 16-minute take.

## Comedy mechanics

1. **Dialogue carries the comedy; narration carries the history.** Over half the
   runtime is characters talking. Narrator states the fact straight → cut to the
   characters acting it out absurdly → narrator resumes, deadpan.
2. **Modern voices in ancient mouths.** Generals talk like coworkers, senators
   like a group chat. Anachronism (thoughts and prayers, HR, timesheets) is the
   main joke engine.
3. **The deadpan correction.** Characters claim something; narrator flatly
   contradicts ("It would not be fine.").
4. **Rule of three, third one breaks.** Lists escalate; the last item is absurd.
5. **Interrupted punchline / cut-away.** A line is cut off by a hard cut to the
   consequence.
6. **Named nobodies.** Minor soldiers and senators get names and personalities
   and come back as recurring bits.
7. **Running gags with counters.** A repeated line or on-screen tally that
   changes meaning as the story darkens.
8. **Tone switch.** Silly up to a massacre, then the narrator plays it straight
   for one sentence — the contrast lands the weight.

## Visual grammar

- **Characters:** short pill/peg-shaped bodies, big round heads, dot eyes,
  heavy angry eyebrows, small mouths; ~1.5 heads tall. Leaders are recognisable
  by one silhouette feature (helmet plume, beard, baldness, eyepatch).
  Faction colors: Rome = red + bronze; Carthage = purple + white + gold.
- **Sets:** soft painted backgrounds (temple interiors, city streets, plains)
  behind flat characters; slight depth blur.
- **Maps, two kinds:** (a) "strategic" satellite-ish Mediterranean, deep navy
  sea, faction-colored territory fills, small flag tokens, labels; (b) "battle"
  flat ochre/green field, armies as red / white rectangles, thick arrows,
  mountain and tree icons.
- **On-screen text:** big chunky white display type with a hard drop shadow for
  chapter cards; small white outlined dialogue subtitles near the speaker;
  red pointer arrows labeling people and things.
- **Battle stat card:** black screen, battle name, two faction flags, strength
  and losses in two columns.
- **Messages** fly across maps as little envelopes; a speaker's portrait pops
  in a circle on a map.

## Our original take (how we differ)

- Own character designs: rounder "bean" bodies, thicker ink outline, our own
  faces; Hannibal = purple crest + short black beard (eyepatch from Act 2);
  Scipio the Younger = big ears + red cloak; Rome's senate = identical bald
  old men.
- Own recurring cast: **Gisgo** (Hannibal's anxious officer — a real officer
  from Plutarch's Cannae anecdote), **Surus** (Hannibal's elephant — named in
  Roman sources), **Hanno** (Carthage's anti-Barca senator, says "No."),
  **Senator Flaccus** (Rome's "Okay. More legions." guy).
- Own running gags: elephant counter, "Okay. More legions.", Hanno's "No.",
  consul counter, Rome copying other people's homework (ships → tactics).
